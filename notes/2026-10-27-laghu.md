# Laghu 152M: speed and quality

Date: 2026-10-27

[Laghu](https://huggingface.co/udgamlabs/Laghu-MLX) is a 152M-parameter open-weight model from Udgam Labs, trained from scratch. I ran it through a fixed launch-day harness: quality on the official BF16 MLX build, and speed on both BF16 and a 4-bit copy I made. All credit for the model goes to Udgam Labs. It's Apache 2.0.

## The setup

These runs were on a different Mac from my usual Mac mini: an Apple M4 Pro (16 GPU cores) with 24 GB, macOS 27.0.1. mlx 0.32.4, mlx-lm 0.32.2, Python 3.12.

## The 4-bit copy

`mlx_lm.convert -q --q-bits 4 --q-group-size 64`. That's 4.506 bits per weight, and the weights file is about 86 MB against 303.6 MB for BF16. It's on Hugging Face at [pratikgx/Laghu-MLX-4bit](https://huggingface.co/pratikgx/Laghu-MLX-4bit).

Udgam Labs chose not to publish a 4-bit build because it clearly loses quality. They report a mean KL of 0.337 and 69.9% top-1 agreement against their F16 reference. I didn't run quality tests on my 4-bit copy, so use the BF16 build for quality.

## How I measured

**Speed** (`scripts/launch-bench/bench_speed.py`): greedy decoding, prompts of 512, 2,048, 8,192 and 16,000 tokens taken from *Pride and Prejudice*, 256 generated tokens with EOS ignored, prefill step 2048. 1 warmup, then 3 timed runs, and the tables show the median. Memory is MLX peak memory.

**Quality**, on BF16, using fixed random subsets (seed 0):

- GSM8K test, 100 questions, 0-shot chat. The answer is the last number in the reply, and replies cut off at 512 tokens count as wrong. Run with thinking off and on.
- IFEval, 100 prompts, thinking off, up to 1,024 tokens, scored with the lm-eval IFEval checkers.
- ARC-Easy test, 200 questions, and HellaSwag validation, 200 items, both 0-shot loglikelihood with no chat template.

The subsets are small, so the error bars are wide (around 5 to 10 points at 100 items).

## Results

| Task | Result | Udgam Labs' number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GSM8K, thinking off | 5.0% | none |
| GSM8K, thinking on | 10.0% | 7.9% (with thinking) |
| IFEval, prompt-level strict / loose | 0.29 / 0.30 | 26.1 |
| IFEval, instruction-level strict / loose | 0.386 / 0.405 | none |
| ARC-Easy acc / acc_norm | 0.585 / 0.49 | none |
| HellaSwag acc / acc_norm | 0.305 / 0.38 | none |

On IFEval, 45% of replies hit the 1,024-token cap because it tends to loop under greedy decoding.

| Prompt tokens | BF16 decode | BF16 prefill | BF16 time to first token | BF16 peak memory | 4-bit decode | 4-bit prefill | 4-bit peak memory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 512 | 364 tok/s | 11,516 tok/s | 0.045 s | 0.66 GB | 402 tok/s | 11,844 tok/s | 0.38 GB |
| 2,048 | 356 tok/s | 11,264 tok/s | 0.18 s | 1.17 GB | 409 tok/s | 10,825 tok/s | 1.11 GB |
| 8,192 | 328 tok/s | 9,198 tok/s | 0.89 s | 1.23 GB | 397 tok/s | 8,876 tok/s | 1.15 GB |
| 16,000 | 303 tok/s | 7,554 tok/s | 2.12 s | 1.26 GB | 379 tok/s | 7,069 tok/s | 1.15 GB |

4-bit decodes about 10 to 25% faster, and prefill is about the same. Peak memory is only lower at short prompts, because the KV cache takes over at longer ones.

Udgam Labs reports about 319 tok/s for BF16 generation on a Mac mini with an Apple M6 and 24 GB.
